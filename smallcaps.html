RESEARCH NOTE 002 · OCTOBER 22, 2026

Small caps, 20 names at a time

Five ideas with strong published evidence, built so a small account could actually hold them. None survived.

All five failed the unchanged criteria

Every idea fell short of the factor-alpha requirement (t ≥ 1.5), and every one had a worst drop beyond the −35% limit. The best return, asset turnover at 8.5% a year, is fully explained by ordinary market and small-company exposure. Nothing advances to the 2023–2025 check or to paper trading.

Why this study

Built to be tradable

  • Small caps suit a small account: lower share prices mean 20 names fit in whole shares.
  • Each idea holds the top 20 stocks on one signal, equally weighted, reranked at every month-end and traded at the next open.
  • Universe: US common stocks outside the S&P 500, priced at least $5 and above the NYSE 20th size percentile.

How it was tested

  • Rules fixed before any results; every outcome reported, including failures.
  • Sealed data physically cut off at the end of 2019; code ran sandboxed; three engines had to agree on every trade.
  • Each idea ran at normal and doubled trading costs, 1998–2019.
  • 2023–2025 has never been examined for small caps and was kept back for any survivor.

What it means

  • Twenty names is too few for these signals. The published factors are broad portfolios of hundreds of stocks. Taking only the 20 most extreme names concentrated risk (worst drops of 56–88%) without keeping the edge.
  • Momentum was the worst. The 20 biggest recent winners are the most volatile small caps; the portfolio lost 88% at its worst and turned over about 13 times a year.
  • Gross profitability did not carry over. At about 10% of the universe it had alpha t 0.97 in note 001; at 20 names it fell to −0.23. The top names by gross profit per dollar of assets are low-asset businesses, not the high-margin firms the profitability factor rewards.
  • Costs are not the main problem. Doubling costs lowered alpha t further, but the edge was missing before costs.

What would be worth trying next

  • Broader portfolios (50–100 names) of the same signals, which match the published evidence better. A small account would need fractional shares or more capital to hold them.
  • Combining signals into one ranking instead of using any single one.
  • Any new version must be registered before it runs, and none of these failures can be reused as fresh evidence.

* One run, momentum at doubled costs, was rejected because two engines disagreed by two millionths of a share on a 15-million-share position. That is a rounding issue in the engine check, not the strategy, and it does not change the verdict.
