COMPANY RESEARCH

Beacon asks whether an AI analyst’s ratings tell you anything.

For each company it assembles the facts that were public on a chosen date, asks two models for a rating, and checks the ratings against what the stock did afterward. The dates are chosen after the models’ training cutoffs so they cannot simply remember the outcome.

Phase 1 · one batch scored, a second in progress

So far the ratings are inconclusive.

In the first batch both models’ rank correlation with later returns (IC) was about 0.05 on 60 companies, with a confidence range that includes zero. The pre-registered bar for “encouraging” was above 0.10. The models also mostly answered “hold,” which limits what the test can show.

How a batch works

  1. ScreenPoint-in-time SEC XBRL fundamentals rank S&P 500 companies on a quality composite. The top 60 are rated.
  2. Fact sheetFinancials plus the latest 10-K and 10-Q text, built twice: once with the company name and once with it hidden.
  3. FreezeThe universe, prompts, models and success rules are hashed and recorded before any rating is made.
  4. RateClaude and Codex each give 3-month and 12-month ratings. Any answer that used tools or commands is rejected.
  5. ScoreRatings are compared with later returns, the screen’s own top 10 and random baskets of the same size.
  6. BenchmarkValue Line’s published Timeliness rank is recorded for the same names as an outside reference.

What would count as success

Phase 1

Encouraging if IC is above 0.10 and the AI’s top 10 beats both the screen’s top 10 and the 75th percentile of random baskets. An IC below zero means stop treating the ratings as a signal.

Long run

Monthly batches for 24 months. IC above 0.03 in at least 60% of months. If IC is zero or negative at 18 months, retire the ratings.

Memory check

Named and hidden-name ratings must correlate at 0.5 or more, otherwise the results are treated as possibly contaminated.

Limits

Sixty companies over ten weeks is a small sample, and these companies were chosen by a quality screen, not at random. One batch cannot show skill; the monthly series is what will. Fundamentals come from SEC filings, so they may differ slightly from other vendors’ versions. Per-company ratings are not published.
