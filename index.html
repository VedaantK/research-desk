What changed and whyCrash protection, alpha test, holdout, crisis results

The original rules rejected any stock strategy that fell more than 35%, which meant any strategy invested through 2008, and asked three holdout years to clear a fixed Sharpe target.

Crash protection

A long stock strategy may be held with one fixed rule: if the S&P 500 ends a month below its 200-day average, move to T-bills until a month ends back above it. The −35% limit applies to the portfolio as held. The rule is never tuned per strategy.

An edge of its own

The rule alone makes the index look good, so a strategy must also beat the six-factor model on its own picks (alpha t-stat ≥ 1.5), measured without protection. Holding the index with the rule cannot pass.

Holdout: did it break?

The later years fail a strategy only when it does clearly worse than its research predicted; a pass can still include losing years. A pass means paper trading, not proof: no strategy kept a factor edge after its research years.

How an idea is testedThe pipeline, data and statistics
  1. DescribeA strategy in plain English, with the reason it should work.
  2. FormalizeClaude writes a precise spec: universe, signals, timing, sizing, exits.
  3. ImplementCodex writes the code. A validator replays it on synthetic markets to catch lookahead.
  4. Backtest ×3A reference engine, vectorbt and NautilusTrader must reconcile on the same orders.
  5. CritiqueClaude and Codex each propose falsifiable fixes. At most two rounds, and every version is counted.
  6. GatesSharpe ≥ 0.5 and worst drop ≥ −35% as held, a six-factor alpha t ≥ 1.5, then doubled costs and nearby settings (Sharpe ≥ 0.3).
  7. HoldoutA separate stretch of history checks that the result did not break. Years already looked at never count as fresh evidence again.
  8. PaperSurvivors trade on an Interactive Brokers paper account before any real money.

Data timing and revisions

CRSP daily prices including delisted companies and delisting returns, point-in-time S&P 500 membership, and Compustat fundamentals used with availability lags. Waiting for the report date does not undo later corrections, so these numbers may not be exactly what was first published. The annual research page lists its own data limits.

Realistic execution

Decisions are made at the close and filled at the next open. Costs include commissions, the quoted spread, size-based market impact and a borrow fee on shorts. Cash earns no interest, which understates hedged strategies.

Honest statistics

Pass/fail rules are set before results are seen. They changed once (version 2, above), written down before any re-scoring, and re-scored verdicts are labelled. Alpha is measured against six factors and every version tried is recorded. The engine refuses to read data past the allowed date, and the annual study went further: its data was physically cut off at the end of 2019 and its code ran sandboxed.
