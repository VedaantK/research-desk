What was tested

The strategy

  • US liquid stocks outside the historical S&P 500.
  • Keep the cheaper half by common book equity / market value.
  • Buy the most profitable fifth of those, ranked by gross profit / assets.
  • Rebalance each June; equal weights, 80% target gross exposure.
  • Use the prior calendar year's fiscal-year data; trade at the next open.

How to read this evidence

  • 1998–2019 historical research; the baseline had already been examined.
  • Annual Compustat data may contain later revisions.
  • Three subperiod tests restart from cash with fixed settings; they are not fresh out-of-sample forecasts.
  • Older S&P-only results on the main desk are separate experiments with different data and rules.

What could improve the research?

These are proposed next steps after reviewing the results. No new strategy tests or parameter search were run for this review.

1. Use a matched benchmark

Test an equal-weight portfolio from the same eligible stocks, with the same timing, exposure and costs. The S&P 500 is not a matched universe for these non-S&P stocks. Six-factor alpha already controls for broad exposures, but this benchmark would make the stock-selection comparison clearer.

Feasible with existing data; untested.

2. Diagnose risk before changing rules

Review position concentration and losses during drawdowns. A fixed lower-exposure or broader-selection test could investigate risk, but reducing exposure does not by itself establish a stock-picking edge. Do not choose a setting just because it makes the historical drawdown pass.

Feasible with existing data; untested.

3. Strengthen the data checks

Audit financial availability and the three listing-gap exits in the value control. Industry-neutral ranking would need historical industry labels; the sealed names table does not contain them. First-reported validation would need historical fundamental vintages.

Partly feasible; missing data remains a limit.

What this review does not justify

  • Promoting profitability alone as a validated winner: its alpha t-statistic is 0.97, below the unchanged 1.5 requirement, and its drawdown is 42.59%.
  • Tuning value cutoffs or adding a trend filter until a historical test passes.
  • Treating cheaper execution as the main fix: doubled costs reduced annual returns by only 0.15–0.23 percentage points across the three portfolios.
  • Calling previously examined history a fresh holdout. Any follow-up requires a fixed protocol and all attempts must remain visible.

FOLLOW-UP · OCTOBER 22, 2026

Why did the earlier quarterly screen look better?

The unfinished small & mid cap screen on the main desk (quarterly data, monthly, 2000–2022, one engine) showed alpha t 2.37; this study shows −0.24. Three fixed runs changed one difference at a time, on the same sealed yearly data, three engines each. Quarterly freshness, the fuller book-equity formula, restated quarterly values and 2020–22 cannot be tested on this data.

  • The start date barely matters (alpha t −0.24 → −0.02).
  • Monthly reranking helps only before costs: alpha t 0.77, falling to 0.01 at doubled costs as turnover rises to about 290% a year.
  • The screen's own rules explain about half the gap (alpha t 1.19), but still fail the 1.5 bar, still fall about 60% at worst, and drop to 0.67 at doubled costs.
  • The rest is untested here. The remaining gap must come from quarterly data, the fuller book-equity formula, restated values or 2020–22. If restated values are the cause, part of the screen's result was look-ahead.

Either way, neither version is worth trading.

Limits and next decision

  • The current version stops at the research stage. A new hypothesis needs a reason and criteria fixed before testing.
  • Annual data availability lags do not remove all restatement bias.
  • The original baseline includes one listing-gap exit and the value-only control includes three; the missing across-gap return remains a data limitation.
