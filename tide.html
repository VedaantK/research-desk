INTRADAY PATTERN RESEARCH

Tide looks for repeatable moves inside the trading day.

It tests defined entry patterns, measures what prices do afterward, and compares exit rules on data kept separate for development, validation, and a final holdout.

What is already built

Setup research

Exits and validation

Measures forward returns and favorable/adverse excursions, compares stop and target rules, and separates development, validation, and final test months. Matched random entries provide a baseline.

Execution bridge

Prepared signals can run through Horizon’s intraday simulator, which models prior-minute quotes, costs, gaps, session exits, and conservative handling when a bar touches both stop and target.

What is needed before a study can run

1. Licensed intraday data

At least 12 months of regular-session trades and consolidated quotes for a point-in-time large-cap universe, plus SPY and QQQ. Twelve months supports Tide’s initial 6-month development, 3-month validation, and 3-month untouched test split; longer history is needed to judge different market regimes.

Your WRDS TAQ access covers the needed source. Export the relevant dates and symbols, then retain the source and cleaning details with the study.

2. Clean identifiers and timestamps

Trade corrections, sale conditions, and quote validity must be handled before import. Data needs exchange timestamps, a consistent ticker-to-PERMNO map, split-aware price-scale breaks, complete XNYS sessions, and coverage checks including early closes.

3. Matching historical reference data

Tide uses Horizon’s CRSP prices and historical S&P 500 membership to avoid selecting today’s winners for an older study. The local CRSP daily file currently ends on December 31, 2025. Refresh it and membership data for any study window beyond that date.

4. Execution assumptions

Set and record realistic commissions, spread, slippage, and market impact. Short setups also need separate historical borrow and locate evidence before they can pass the advancement gate.

Path from data to findings

  1. Choose dates and make a sample exportStart with one regular session and a few symbols, including SPY, to confirm the WRDS columns and quote coverage.
  2. Clean and map the inputsProduce minute bars with OHLCV and valid NBBO, then check ticker identities, corporate actions, session coverage, and source history.
  3. Run the bounded studyBuild the point-in-time universe, calculate signals and excursions, and compare exit rules with transaction costs.
  4. Check unseen periodsReview matched baselines, walk-forward periods, final holdout results, per-stock performance, and nearby parameter settings.
  5. Review candidatesInspect the generated report and configuration. Tide labels candidates for manual review; it does not promote them automatically.
  6. Paper trade separatelyOnly candidates that pass predefined evidence checks should move to paper execution and live-simulation monitoring.
